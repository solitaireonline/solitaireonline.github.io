    Solitaire is a single-player card game where the objective is to move all cards to the foundation piles. While the classic version is known as Klondike Solitaire, there are several variations available, including Klondike Turn 3 and FreeCell. The game is also commonly known as "Patience," a name that reflects the strategy and persistence often required to complete a game successfully.

    You can play Solitaire online directly in your browser on desktop and mobile devices without downloading or installing anything.

    Objective

    The goal is to move all cards to the four foundation piles. Each foundation must be built by suit in ascending order, starting with an Ace and ending with a King. To achieve this, you will move and organize face-up cards from the tableau while drawing additional cards from the stock pile when needed.

    Solitaire Setup

    Setting up Solitaire is straightforward. The game is played using four main areas, each with a specific role in helping you organize cards and build the foundation piles.

    • The Tableau

    The tableau is the primary playing area and consists of seven columns containing a total of 28 cards. The columns are arranged from left to right, with the first column holding one card, the second holding two, and so on until the seventh column contains seven cards. Only the top card in each column is face-up, while the remaining cards stay face-down until uncovered during gameplay.

    • The Stock Pile

    The remaining 24 cards form the stock pile. These cards are placed face-down and are used when no more moves are available on the tableau. Cards are drawn from the stock pile one at a time.

    • The Waste Pile

    When cards are drawn from the stock pile, they are placed face-up in the waste pile. The top card of the waste pile can be played onto the tableau or moved to a foundation pile when a valid move is available.

    • The Foundation Piles

    The four foundation piles are where all cards must ultimately be placed to win the game. Each foundation is built by suit in ascending order, beginning with an Ace and continuing through to the King.

    Solitaire Rules

    To win Solitaire, you must organize all cards into the foundation piles by following a set of basic rules and card movement patterns.

    • Only face-up cards can be moved. Face-down cards in the tableau remain unavailable until the card covering them is removed. Playing a card from the waste pile may also reveal a new playable card.
    • Move cards between tableau columns by placing a card onto another card of the opposite color and one rank higher. For example, a 6 of Clubs can be placed on a 7 of Hearts or 7 of Diamonds.
    • Move groups of correctly sequenced cards together. The top card of the sequence must be placed on a card of the opposite color that is one rank higher.
    • Reveal face-down cards whenever possible. When the face-up card covering a face-down card is moved, the hidden card is turned face-up and becomes available for play.
    • Build the foundation piles starting with Aces. Cards must be added in ascending order and match the suit of the foundation pile. For example, the Ace of Spades is followed by the 2 of Spades, then the 3 of Spades, and so on.
    • Empty tableau columns can only be filled with a King or a valid sequence that begins with a King.
    • Draw cards from the stock pile when no moves are available on the tableau. Continue drawing until you find a playable card for the tableau or foundation piles.
    • Reuse the stock pile when all cards have been moved to the waste pile. The waste pile can be recycled back into the stock pile and used again.

    Following these rules carefully will help you uncover hidden cards, create more move options, and complete the foundation piles more efficiently.

    Strategies to Win Solitaire

    Improve your chances of winning by following these helpful Solitaire strategies:

    • Reveal face-down cards as early as possible. The more hidden cards you uncover, the more moves and opportunities you will have.
    • Check the first card in the stock pile before making your opening move. It may help create a sequence or reveal additional plays.
    • Study the tableau before making decisions. Looking ahead can help you avoid moves that limit future options.
    • Move Aces and 2s to the foundation piles whenever possible. Building your foundations early creates more space for organizing cards.
    • Prioritize larger tableau columns. These columns contain more hidden cards, making them the most valuable areas to clear.
    • Think carefully before moving cards to the foundation piles. Some cards may be more useful in the tableau for building sequences and uncovering face-down cards.
    • Don't be afraid to move cards back from the foundation piles when it helps create better opportunities on the tableau.
    • Try to empty tableau columns whenever possible. Empty spaces can be filled with Kings or sequences that begin with a King, giving you more flexibility.
    • Use hint and undo features if they are available. They can help you spot missed moves and test different strategies.

    Success in Solitaire comes from planning ahead, revealing hidden cards, and creating as many move options as possible throughout the game.

    How Difficult Is Solitaire?

    Classic Klondike Solitaire is generally considered an easy card game to learn, making it a popular choice for both beginners and experienced players. While the rules are straightforward, winning consistently requires planning, strategy, and careful card management.

    Turn 1 Solitaire is the easier version because only one card is drawn from the stock pile at a time, giving players more opportunities to make useful moves. Turn 3 Solitaire is more challenging, as three cards are drawn at once, reducing the number of available options and requiring more strategic decision-making.

    What Is the Difference Between Turn 1 and Turn 3 Solitaire?

    The main difference lies in how cards are drawn from the stock pile.

    • Turn 1 Solitaire: One card is revealed from the stock pile at a time, making it easier to access the cards you need.
    • Turn 3 Solitaire: Three cards are revealed at once, making it harder to reach specific cards and increasing the overall difficulty.

    If you're new to Solitaire, Turn 1 is a great place to start. Players looking for a more strategic challenge often prefer Turn 3 Solitaire.
